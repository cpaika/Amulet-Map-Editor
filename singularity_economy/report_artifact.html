Thesis as given: software singularity in 2027, general-purpose robotics ramping from 2028. Seventeen research agents swept every affected vertical; a calibrated bottleneck simulation ran 800 Monte-Carlo economies; 45 candidate trades were then attacked by adversarial reviewers. 20 survived.

01What the model robustly says

Which constraint caps the AI buildout, by year

Share of 800 Monte-Carlo runs in which each constraint binds. Hover a column for exact shares.

Power (turbines, transformers, queues) Chips (CoWoS, HBM, EUV) Capital willingness Demand / adoption friction
  1. Power is the constraint of the decade. It binds in 84% of runs in 2026 and 60–81% every year through 2033. Evidence: gas turbines sold out through 2030 (GE Vernova selling 2031 slots at +10–20% pricing), transformer lead times 3–5 years, switchgear sold out through 2028, interconnection queues 5–10 years. Scarcity rents concentrate in energized capacity and the equipment that creates it.
  2. Chips get a secondary window, 2027–2030 — CoWoS packaging, HBM stacking, EUV output — then capacity catches up. Historical law: capacity-scarcity rents die 1–3 years after the supply response; IP-moat rents (TSMC, ASML, CUDA) persist for decades.
  3. Capital becomes the binding constraint late (2034+) — the boom ends with financing discipline, not demand exhaustion. Early symptoms exist now: Oracle −49.5% on credit fears, Alphabet's first negative-FCF quarter, $250B+ of AI bonds.
  4. The "Cisco moment" (capex growth <15%) has a median arrival of 2036 under the thesis (p10: 2034). If the singularity is real, picks-and-shovels have years of runway; the early-deceleration tail is the fizzle worlds, and scenario weights — not top-timing — carry that risk.
  5. Casualty decay is back-half loaded: median cognitive-work displacement ~7% (2028) → ~25% (2030) → ~50% (2032); IT-services pool −32% by 2033. History says casualty multiples die 2–4 years before revenue — under the thesis, mid-2026 is early, which is when shorts must be placed.
  6. Robotics is a component story now, a labor story later. Median 2032 robot production is ~0.8M units/yr; physical-labor displacement stays under 5% through 2036 — yet pure plays at 145–400x already price the p90 outcome.
  7. Transition-recession tail: world GDP growth goes negative in ~10% of runs. The gated credit shorts and the steepener hedge this tail.

02What the market already prices

  • Crowding sits in semis, not in multiples: NVDA ~21x forward, TSM 19.5x — the risk is the E in a fizzle, not the P/E. Record hedge-fund chip exposure, but the Mag-7 already corrected in March 2026.
  • The punished capex payers are the neglected longs: MSFT −25% over a year to 20.6x, GOOGL sold off for raising capex, ORCL halved. If 2027 agent revenue vindicates 2026 capex, this is where the asymmetry lives.
  • First-order casualty shorts are over: Chegg at $0.97, Concentrix at 1.9x forward, Salesforce −49%. Remaining short alpha sits in names still priced as durable — though the red team then killed most single-name expressions of even this (ADP's elasticity is empirically <0.5; MAN is a 19.9%-SI squeeze trap). What survived: RHI via spreads, HUBS, CHRW post-print.
  • The bond market prices no singularity (10y 4.71%, negative simple ERP), and S&P short interest is at a record — a hedged melt-up, not euphoria.
  • ~$2T of private AI-lab marks must clear through IPOs (Anthropic Oct 2026, OpenAI 2027) — the cycle's mark-to-market events for the whole complex.

Short TCS — killed (high confidence). Incoherent with the thesis itself: enterprise AI integration is what TCS sells. 2026–28 is a services super-cycle before displacement; the $2.6B AI run-rate is additive now.
Short Atlassian — killed (high confidence). The 125:1 "capture ratio" was a category error: Claude Code ARR is inference spend, not reallocated Jira budget — and Atlassian shipped agent integrations into the coding tools. Fundamentals accelerating.
Short Synchrony — killed (high confidence). Retailer Share Arrangements contractually absorb ~50% of incremental charge-offs — the equity is half-hedged against exactly the scenario being shorted. Purer expression: OMF (no loss-sharing buffer), gated on delinquency data.
Long Nabtesco — killed (high confidence). Product-category error caught by review: humanoids use harmonic/planetary actuators and roller screws, not RV cycloidal reducers sized for 100kg+ industrial arms.
Long Micron — killed at this entry. The blowout is spot commodity DRAM (+700% yoy) — the most mean-reverting price series in semis — not the contracted HBM story; $1.12T cap for a memory company is the cycle marker. Revives on a 30–40% spot-scare drawdown with SCA floors intact.
Long GE Vernova — killed at this price. The confirming quarter contained a 33% EBIT miss, and the FCF guide-raise is substantially slot deposits, not margin. Revives at ~$700–800 or on two consecutive Power-EBIT beats. The franchise is real; the entry isn't.

Short ADP / PAYX — killed. The elasticity assumption was empirically wrong (2009: employment −5%, ADP revenue ~flat — realized elasticity <0.5), and PAYX's SMB base is physical-labor heavy, robotics-gated to 2028+. Revives via Jan-2028 puts only on negative pays-per-control.

The three loss rows are correlated expressions of one bet — the thesis, routed through Taiwan-concentrated supply chains, funded by fragile credit. Size the whole construct against total capital.

  1. The thesis is the book. If capability plateaus, longs de-rate 30–60% (fizzle scenario) and shorts squeeze. The 10% fizzle weight and 12% discount rate are load-bearing.
  2. Taiwan is the anchor position's single point of failure — no intra-sector diversification exists; only sizing and out-of-book hedges.
  3. The levered periphery cracks first (neoclouds, DC SPVs, Oracle credit): a 2027 refinancing accident repriced everything in 2001, including the honest suppliers.
  4. IPO event risk: Anthropic (Oct 2026) and OpenAI (2027) are the mark-to-market events for ~$2T of private value — a discounted print is a de-gross signal.
  5. Crowding asymmetry: in a broad risk-off, longs de-rate faster than casualty shorts fall; both legs lose initially.
  6. Backlogs are deposits, not GAAP revenue — a 2027 digestion year converts "sold out through 2030" into cancellations.
  7. The robotics leg is deliberately unexpressed at inception — every component long died in review. If robotics surprises early, this book lags the thesis; the build trigger is a shipping humanoid program naming a non-China reducer/actuator supplier.
