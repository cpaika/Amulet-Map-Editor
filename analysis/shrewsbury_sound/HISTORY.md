# History of the land at 6 Trowbridge Circle, Shrewsbury, MA

Most recent → earliest. Only the parcel record is documented for *this exact
lot*; earlier eras are neighborhood / area context.

## The lot & house (documented)
- **Built 2001** — an 11-room Colonial single-family home on a **0.66-acre
  lot** (MassGIS Standardized Assessors' Parcels, Level 3; PROP_ID 23_118008).
  Last arm's-length sale **2 Jul 2013, $636,000**; assessed ~**$1.04M**
  (land $299.8k / building $715.8k).
- The neighborhood was built in **three phases**, visible in year-built data:
  - **1964–1971** — oldest cluster, lower Trowbridge Lane (#5–17).
  - **1994–1998** — main subdivision, most of Trowbridge Lane (Colonials, ~0.3 ac).
  - **2000–2002** — the **Trowbridge Circle cul-de-sac** (#5–11, incl. #6), the
    final, more upscale phase, larger lots (0.46–0.70 ac).
- So this lot was undeveloped until the late-1990s subdivision wave; the
  cul-de-sac itself was platted/built last (~2000–01). The recorded plan and
  developer name live at the Worcester County Registry of Deeds / Shrewsbury
  Planning Board (not online).

## 20th century (area context)
Before suburban subdivision this inland tract was almost certainly **woodland /
former farm or market-garden land** — it sat back from the water and so missed
the Lake Quinsigamond recreation boom (summer cottages, boat clubs, regattas,
the **White City amusement park, 1905–1960**, and the lakeshore trolley line),
which was concentrated on the shoreline a couple of km west. The uniform
Colonial buildout and late dates fit open land developed straight to suburb.

## 18th–19th century (area context)
Shrewsbury was a **farming town** of orchards and market gardens (its farmers
later supplied the lake resorts). Large tracts were granted from the 1660s.
The **Trowbridge** street name is a common New England surname; no source ties
it to a specific owner of this tract.

## Colonial & Indigenous (regional context)
The land was **Nipmuc** territory. **"Quinsigamond"** is Algonquian, usually
translated **"pickerel/fishing place."** In **1664** Nipmuc leaders (incl. Peter
Jethro) deeded Quinsigamond-area land to settlers; **King Philip's War
(1675–76)** depopulated the region; a **1717 grant** created Shrewsbury, settled
~1720–22 and **incorporated 19 Dec 1727** (named for Shrewsbury, England).

## Earliest record for *this* land
The house (2001) and the late-1990s subdivision that created the lot. Beyond
that, only area-wide records apply — the colonial chain back to the **1717 town
grant** and the **1664 Nipmuc deed**, neither demonstrably covering this exact
tract. A full pre-1990s chain of title would require deed-tracing at the
Worcester Registry of Deeds and Shrewsbury Historical Society holdings (e.g.
the 1870 Beers Atlas of Worcester County).
